SPF, DKIM & DMARC Explained: How to Stop Your Emails Going to Spam

If your business emails land in spam — or criminals send fake emails “from” your domain — these three DNS records are the fix. Here is what each one does and how to set them up safely.

By Abdul Rafey31 October 2026

Why this matters now

Email was never designed to prove who sent a message. Anyone can put ceo@yourcompany.com in the From line. To fight spoofing and spam, mailbox providers check three DNS records on your domain — and since 2024 Gmail and Yahoo require them from bulk senders, while unauthenticated mail from smaller senders is increasingly filtered too.

SPF — who is allowed to send

SPF (Sender Policy Framework) is a TXT record listing the servers allowed to send email for your domain. For a domain that sends through Google Workspace:

Type: TXT   Name: @
v=spf1 include:_spf.google.com ~all
  • Add an include: for every service that sends as you (your email provider, newsletter tool, CRM, invoicing app).
  • A domain must have only one SPF record — merge them instead of adding a second.
  • SPF allows at most 10 DNS lookups; too many includes make the whole record fail.
  • ~all (soft fail) is a safe start; -all rejects everything not listed.

DKIM — a tamper-proof signature

DKIM (DomainKeys Identified Mail) adds a cryptographic signature to every message. Your provider signs mail with a private key; receivers check it against a public key you publish in DNS. If the message was altered in transit, the check fails.

Type: TXT   Name: google._domainkey
v=DKIM1; k=rsa; p=MIIBIjANBgkqhkiG9w0BAQEFAAOCAQ8A...

You do not write this record yourself: generate it in your email provider’s admin console (for Google Workspace: Admin → Apps → Gmail → Authenticate email), publish it, then switch signing on. Use 2048-bit keys where offered.

DMARC — the policy that ties it together

DMARC tells receivers what to do when a message fails SPF and DKIM alignment — meaning the domain that passed must match the domain in the visible From address — and where to send reports.

Type: TXT   Name: _dmarc
v=DMARC1; p=none; rua=mailto:dmarc-reports@yourcompany.com

A safe rollout plan

  1. Inventory every service that sends email as your domain.
  2. Publish SPF and enable DKIM for each of them.
  3. Start DMARC at p=none — monitoring only — and read the aggregate reports for two to four weeks.
  4. Fix any legitimate sender that fails.
  5. Tighten to p=quarantine (failures go to spam), then p=reject (failures are blocked).
Jumping straight to p=reject can block your own invoices or newsletters. Monitor first, enforce second.

Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender rules

If you send large volumes to personal Gmail or Yahoo inboxes (Google’s line is around 5,000 messages a day), you must authenticate with SPF and DKIM, publish DMARC with alignment, offer one-click unsubscribe in marketing mail, and keep spam complaints below 0.3%. Below that volume the rules are softer — but authenticated mail still lands in the inbox far more reliably.

How to check your domain

dig +short TXT yourcompany.com
dig +short TXT _dmarc.yourcompany.com
dig +short TXT google._domainkey.yourcompany.com

In Gmail you can also open any message you sent, choose Show original, and confirm SPF, DKIM and DMARC all say PASS.
